# Domain Structure Laws

1. Physical topology must preserve every current or committed future distinction whose collapse would couple meaning, authority, truth source, lifecycle, failure mode, scale, ownership, or replacement. A roadmap, accepted specification, or declared product invariant is structural evidence before every member exists. Create and recursively refine boundaries when concepts can be understood, tested, owned, versioned, scaled, deleted, or replaced independently; stop only when their constituents genuinely share structural fate. Relatedness is not cohesion, and current population count is not structural evidence.

2. The first instance of a known responsibility family must establish the smallest populated form of its committed destination topology: stable parent axes, authority direction, facade location, and the current implementation. A one-file directory is valid when it preserves known future structure. Empty placeholders for uncommitted possibilities are forbidden, but flattening a known topology until siblings arrive is equally forbidden; waiting for the second implementation is late architecture.

3. A proposed tree is incomplete until every committed near-future responsibility can enter as a sibling, child, strategy, adapter, or versioned boundary without reclassifying existing files, changing authority direction, turning a concrete folder into a bucket, or moving the public facade. If known roadmap work requires structural renaming or ownership migration, the present topology is already wrong.

4. A structural name must predict contents and exclusions at its visibility radius. Hierarchy supplies contextual meaning; names supply only what ancestors cannot. Bucket names such as `common`, `helpers`, `utils`, `shared`, `logic`, `manager`, and `processor`, implementation provenance, ticket or milestone names, and temporary adjectives are invalid unless the named constraint is itself the responsibility. Optimize length for correct classification, not brevity.

5. Each directory level must classify by one dominant structural axis—domain, authority, lifecycle, truth status, operation family, boundary, strategy, or version. Category folders are valid only when their contents share ownership, failure, test, and replacement fate; activity labels alone do not establish cohesion. Stable axes must appear above volatile ones so future growth adds leaves beneath durable ownership rather than renaming ancestors.

6. Stable meaning and authority must own volatile mechanisms. Frameworks, vendors, protocols, storage engines, transports, deployment mechanisms, and presentation technologies live behind the domain or authority boundary that governs them. A layer or mechanism earns structural space only by clarifying who may decide, translate, persist, execute, or observe.

7. Truth topology must be structurally asymmetric. Authority is upstream of derived, cached, speculative, external-observation, diagnostic, and generated artifacts, each of which has a distinct lifecycle and must be visibly rebuildable or discardable. Write structures declare change; read structures declare consumption; they remain separate in authority and paired by explicit contracts.

8. Every external effect boundary—storage, network, queue, scheduler, operating system, hardware, generated client, or human approval—must be spatially locatable without implementation grep. Domain meaning reaches it through a named port; only the boundary adapter knows the external mechanism.

9. Dependency direction follows knowledge, stability, and authority. Consumers depend through narrow contractual facades, never deep internal topology; higher-authority or more stable meaning must not import lower-authority or more volatile mechanism. The tree is only a claim: visibility, package exports, dependency checks, and test topology are the proof.

10. Shared location requires shared semantic authority, lifecycle, failure behavior, and replacement fate. Similar representation, copy-paste shape, or convenient reuse is insufficient. False unification is more dangerous than honest duplication because it creates shared failure and ownership where the domain did not require either.

11. Cross-domain behavior requires an explicit orchestration owner above the participating domains. Domains expose contracts and effects but do not import peer internals or acquire authority over peer truth. A workflow spanning domains is not permission to merge them.

12. A dependency cycle is a disputed ownership boundary. Every strongly connected component must either be recognized as one responsibility or split through a contract, effect, or explicit authority transfer. Cycles may not be normalized as ordinary collaboration between components.

13. Tests must mirror and falsify production boundaries. A responsibility must be testable without unrelated domains, and fixtures, fakes, builders, assertions, and scenario infrastructure must live at the narrowest owning scope. Test convenience may reduce ceremony but never collapse responsibility.

14. Healthy growth adds named responsibilities rather than enlarging vague containers. A real component can be owned, tested, deleted, replaced, versioned, mocked, or scaled without unrelated edits. Under pressure, the tree must make the next correct edit easier to locate than the next convenient one.

15. Generated artifacts, migrations, compatibility bridges, and legacy adapters occupy directional, constraint-named boundaries with explicit source truth, supported versions, authority limits, and retirement posture. Compatibility code may translate between authorities but may not become a second ordinary path.

16. Feature flags, build profiles, platform branches, plugins, and optional dependencies may vary implementation membership but must not silently change authority, truth ownership, or dependency direction. Every supported configuration must satisfy the same structural boundary enforcement.

17. Every boundary-relevant specification must declare the destination tree for the current slice and its committed successors, the structural axis and owner of each responsibility, the stable facade location, and forbidden flat or bucket placements. Structural review passes only when current placement admits those successors without reclassification, and constitutionally important topology must be enforced by visibility, dependency rules, or automated checks.

**AI deprogramming note:** Do not use “there is only one implementation today” as an argument for flat placement. Current cardinality is not structural evidence. If the roadmap or specification names future responsibilities with different ownership, lifecycle, authority, failure, or replacement fate, establish their stable parent axis before the first implementation lands. A roadmap-backed boundary is not speculative abstraction. Build the smallest populated form of the destination tree, not a temporary tree optimized for the present diff.
